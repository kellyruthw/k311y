## Ant Farm

A multiplayer ant colony social simulation for the browser, built in PixiJS — equal parts digital terrarium and personality experiment.

Every ant gets a generated personality (MBTI-style trait axes crossed with a D&D-style alignment), a capability system where experience gates what an ant can even attempt, relationship scores that shift as ants collide and interact, and — because every society needs its problems — an evil act system. I wrote the full technical design document: personality generation, capability gating and caps, collision and relationship logic, role assignment, an 88+ question companion bank, and the notification voice of the colony itself.

Ant Farm is also where I learned my most important lesson about building with AI — the moment I realized the codebase had drifted past my comprehension, and restructured it until another engineer could walk in and follow it. The colony got more organized; so did the repo.

## Star Crossed

A cozy, nocturnal mobile game concept: collect stars, fuse them in the Star Lab, and arrange them on a constellation canvas — with an I-Spy minigame and a stardust economy holding it together.

This one became a full design-system exercise. I built the CSS token system, the component library, and interactive screen mockups (the Sky canvas, the Star Lab fusion flow, the Codex) — and then documented the whole thing in a DESIGN-SYSTEM.md written *for Claude Code*, so the AI building alongside me ships components that match the system instead of approximating it. The star roster is its own labor of love: 42 fictional stars, each mapped to real astronomy.

Star Crossed is the clearest picture of my range in one project: game design, visual identity, component architecture, and AI-tooling documentation, all pointed at something soft and glowing.
